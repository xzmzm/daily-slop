How to read a draft

Threading (top left) — one column per warp end, one row per shaft. A mark says "this end goes through a heddle on that shaft."

Tie-up (top right) — one column per treadle. A circle says "pressing this treadle raises that shaft." Rising shed.

Treadling (right) — one row per weft pick, top to bottom, marking which treadle you press.

Drawdown (centre) — computed, never drawn by hand: end e shows on pick p exactly when the treadle for p is tied to the shaft e is threaded on. Everything else is weft.

A float is a thread passing over several others without interlacing. Long floats make satin shiny — and make cloth fall apart.
