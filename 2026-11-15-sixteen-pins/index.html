The ad ran in Electronic News on November 15, 1971: “Announcing a new era in integrated electronics.” The 4004 packed 2,300 transistors behind 16 pins and ran at 740 kHz — and the Busicom 141-PF calculator shipped with one 4004, four 4001 ROMs, two 4002 RAMs and three 4003 shift registers. The toy's timing model: one memory fetch costs ⌈12/w⌉ address periods, ⌈8/w⌉ data periods and 3 execution periods — at four data leads that is exactly the datasheet's 8-period, 10.8 µs instruction cycle. Mnemonics follow the MCS-4 sheet; opcode hex is decorative.
