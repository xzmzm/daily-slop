74 YEARS AGO TODAY · FIRST COMPUTER ELECTION CALL

Early returns.

The polls called 1952 close. At 8:30 pm, with a few percent of the vote in, a UNIVAC told CBS that Eisenhower would win 438 electoral votes. CBS didn’t believe it and kept it off the air. The trick was to compare each early precinct with how the same precinct voted in 1948, instead of reading the raw count.

How the swing works

Each state here has ten reporting units: four city units holding about two-thirds of the vote, and six rural ones. Cities count faster and lean Democratic, so the first returns make Stevenson look strong. The projection ignores that lean. For each reported unit it takes this year’s Republican share minus the same unit’s 1948 share, averages that swing, and adds it to the 1948 share of every unit not yet in. States with no returns borrow the average swing of their region.

What’s invented

Electoral votes are the real 1952 numbers, and state shares are rounded two-party figures for 1948 and 1952. The precincts, reporting times, odds formula and the typo’s location are made up for this page. UNIVAC’s real program, by Max Woodbury, compared returns with 1944 and 1948 and is not reproduced here.
