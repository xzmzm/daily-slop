October 13, 2010 · San José mine, Atacama, Chile

Plan B

A cave-in on August 5 left 33 men in a workshop 622 metres under the Atacama. Three drills raced to reach them — Plan A, Plan B, Plan C — and the hole that arrived first had to be straight enough to hit a room. Keep the bits fresh, keep the holes on line, and when the Fénix capsule starts running, bring all 33 up before the calendar beats you.

Seventeen days of silenceThe men stretched two days of emergency rations to two weeks and drank mine water. On day 17 a probe hole came back with a note taped to the bit: Estamos bien en el refugio, los 33.
Why the hole had to be straightThe workshop was one room off 5 km of ramp. A hole that arrives 20 m to the side is a hole into dead rock. Plan C's fast oil-rig hole kept wandering and had to be pulled and repositioned.
66 centimetresThe winning hole was reamed in stages to 66 cm — room for the 54 cm Fénix capsule and its wheels, and nothing else. The men rode up one at a time, the fittest first, the foreman last.

Rates are calibrated to the real operation: Plan B averaged about 18 m a day net of bit changes and still broke through on October 9. Depth scales are compressed for drawing; the capsule is drawn much larger than 1.9 m against a 624 m hole. Deviation, bit wear, snag positions and rider nerves are modelled stochastically — every shift is a little different.
