Overboard

Weight thrown out early has longer to turn into height. The air sinks hardest in the last miles below the pass.

    Toy model. On 28–29 November 1929 Bernt Balchen, Harold June, Ashley McKinley and Richard Byrd flew from Little America to the South Pole and back. Climbing the glacier to the plateau, the overloaded Trimotor could not gain height, and the crew threw food overboard until it did. Byrd's dispatches and later accounts disagree on the bag weights (125 to 250 lb), but most put the total at about two sacks. The ceiling formula, sink, fuel figures and terrain profile here are made up to fit that story, not measured.
