HOW THE LOOP WORKS

The rule was sound
for one investor.

Each insurer keeps stock worth five times its cushion above a floor at 85% of Friday's wealth. When the market drops 1%, the cushion shrinks by more, so the rule says sell. The desk can only push a little through each minute, so unfilled orders pile up — the faint bars. Every sale moves the price, which widens every other insurer's gap. Bargain hunters buy the discount, but back away while the tape is falling fast. Numbers are a tuned toy, not the Brady Commission's data.
