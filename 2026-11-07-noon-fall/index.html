A NOON IN ALSACE

For the Ensisheim stone, fallen
7 November 1492.

A fireball's fate is a race. Drag squeezes it — dynamic pressure grows with the square of speed while the air below thickens faster than the rock slows. Ablation eats it from the outside. Its strength holds it together, but big bodies are weaker than small ones: the flaws have more room to find each other. Whichever gives up first writes the ending.

Exponential atmosphere (scale height 7.2 km), flat ground, one constant flight angle. Drag coefficient 0.7; 2 % of stagnation heat goes into ablation; 0.5 % of that energy glows; brightness is the apparent magnitude for a watcher 100 km away. Material strengths are 1-metre dynamic strengths with a quarter-power size effect. Dark flight is compressed to fit; the wind, spins and fragment showers are left out.
