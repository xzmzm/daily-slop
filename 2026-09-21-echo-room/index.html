THE SMALL SURPRISE

Softer walls change how much. Not how soon.

Keep the geometry fixed and raise absorption. The reflected spikes get smaller, but their arrival times stay put.

time = path length / 343 m·s⁻¹
What this little room does — and does not — simulate

Mirror-image sources generate specular paths in a rectangular horizontal plane. Travel time is path length / 343 m·s⁻¹. Pressure amplitude is (√(1 − absorption))bounces / max(1 m, path length). This combines planar reflection geometry with a spherical-spreading approximation, not a 2D wave-equation solution. All walls share one frequency-independent energy-absorption value. Exact corner hits are omitted because a unique reflection normal does not exist there.

The sound is an original 24 ms synthetic tap, summed over up to ten reflections with fractional-sample delays. Direct and room modes have the same gain; neither is loudness-normalized. This is mono, not binaural audio. The timeline draws separate path amplitudes, not a measured sound-level response. The animated paths show only orders 0–2 plus the selected path; the sound and timeline include all retained orders. Visual replay is slowed to 0.06×; the sound always plays at real speed. Presets are illustrative, not measured materials or calibrated room designs.
