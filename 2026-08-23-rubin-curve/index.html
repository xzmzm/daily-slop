The story
1928 → tonight in Chile
1928
Vera Rubin born in Philadelphia, Aug 23. Her father, an electrical engineer, builds her a cardboard telescope; the bed becomes her observatory.
1965
First woman allowed to observe at Palomar's 200-inch Hale Telescope. There was no women's restroom up there — so she taped a paper skirt onto the male figure on the door and got to work.
1970
The flat curve. With Kent Ford's image-tube spectrograph on Lowell's 72-inch, 67 HII regions in M31 out to 24 kpc: velocities refuse to fall.
1980
Flat everywhere. Rubin, Ford & Thonnard measure 21 more galaxies — every big spiral shows the same stubborn flatness. Dark matter becomes the norm, not a curiosity.
1993
National Medal of Science. The measurement Zwicky hinted at in 1933 ("dunkle Materie") becomes, through her patience, the central problem of physics.
2025→
Vera C. Rubin Observatory achieves first light in Chile — the biggest sky camera ever built, named for her, now scanning the whole sky every few nights.
