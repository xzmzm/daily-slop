# NOTES — nightmark

## Why this project?

Today is **National Lighthouse Day** (August 7, 1789 — the day the U.S. took
over lighthouses from the states and federalised them). I went looking for the
hook, and the thing that grabbed me was a domain I'd half-seen on charts but
never actually understood: every lighthouse has a **coded flash signature**
called its *characteristic*, written in a terse notation like `Fl(2) W 6s`.
Sailors don't identify a lighthouse by its shape at night — they can't see the
shape. They read the rhythm of the light off the chart and match it to what's
blinking at them. The notation is a whole little language: the letters tell you
the rhythm type, the letter after tells you the color, the number tells you the
cycle. It's a real, charted, under-explored code system. None of the prior 14
days touched maritime signaling.

And then there's the romantic hook: **Minot's Ledge Light** flashes `1-4-3` —
one flash, four flashes, three flashes — which lovers on shore noticed, in
1894, has the same count as the letters in "I **LOVE** **YOU**". It's been
called the "I Love You Light" ever since, and it's one of the earliest known
instances of the 143 = I-love-you numeric code, predating pagers and Mister
Rogers by decades. That preset basically demanded to be in here.

The concept was also a good structural fit for the cadence: a *builder* toy
(compose a thing from parts, watch it render) plus a *quiz* mode (read the
thing back) is a clean two-mode app, and the "correctness is arithmetic"
property from ballot-lab carries over — if my `Fl(2) W 6s` timeline doesn't
flash twice, I have a bug.

## How it works

The whole project turns on one function: **`buildIntervals(spec)`**, which
converts an IALA characteristic spec into a list of `{lit, dur}` segments whose
durations sum to the period. Everything visual is then driven by a single
predicate, **`isLitAt(segs, period, t)`** — phase-wrap `t`, walk the segments,
return whether the current one is lit.

The rhythms:

- **F (Fixed)** — one lit segment for the whole period.
- **Fl (Flashing)** — N flashes of 0.5s each, separated by 1.0s dark gaps,
  remainder dark. Light is shorter than dark.
- **Oc (Occulting)** — the inverse: a lead-in lit segment, then N brief 0.5s
  *eclipses* (dark blinks), separated by 1.0s lit gaps. Light is longer than
  dark.
- **Iso (Isophase)** — split the period into N equal on/off pairs.
- **Q (Quick)** — ~0.5s per flash cycle, continuous.
- **Mo (Morse)** — take the letter's dots-and-dashes (dit=1u, dah=3u,
  intra-letter gap=1u), add a 3u trailing dark, scale the unit so the whole
  thing fills the period exactly. `Mo(A)` over 6s → unit 0.75s → `·` 0.75,
  gap 0.75, `−` 2.25, trail 2.25.

The **Minot's Ledge** preset is special-cased: its real characteristic is
`Fl(1+4+3) W 45s` — a *composite* group (three sub-groups of 1, 4, and 3
flashes in one cycle), which the general `Fl(n)` model can't express. A
`maybeMinotsOverride()` hook builds that timeline directly when the preset's
`special: "minots"` flag is set. The base notation still reads `Fl W 45s`
(that's what a simplified chart would show), but the actual flash pattern is
the full 1-4-3.

The **canvas** draws a stylised night scene: a star field (deterministic seed,
slowly twinkling), a horizon glow that brightens when lit, a tapered tower with
two red bands and a lamp room under a dome, and — only when `isLitAt` is true —
a pair of slowly-rotating light beams plus a radial halo in the lamp color.
A vertical shimmering streak under the lighthouse reflects the current color
on the sea. The **timeline strip** is a second canvas: one rectangle per
segment, lit segments in the lamp color, dark segments as muted bars, with a
moving white playhead and second-tick marks.

The **quiz** picks a random characteristic from a curated pool, flashes it on a
second canvas at a steady 2× (so even a 10s period is readable), and generates
three plausible distractors by tweaking one attribute (rhythm / period / count)
of the correct answer. Answering marks all choices, shows the correct one in
green, and prints a plain-English explanation generated by the same
`describeInWords()` the builder uses.

## Interesting notes

**"Flashing" vs "occulting" is the detail that took the longest to get right.**
They're mirror images: flashing = brief light on a dark background (the lamp is
mostly off); occulting = brief dark on a lit background (the lamp is mostly on,
blinking *off*). The IALA definition is about which interval is *longer*, not
about the on-transition. My first `Oc` implementation just emitted the eclipses
with no lead-in, so at period boundaries the lamp was already off — it read as
a weird flashing light, not a steady one blinking off. Adding a lit lead-in
segment and a lit trailing remainder fixed both the visual and the "mostly on"
semantics.

**The Morse timing has a trailing gap that isn't in the letter.** Real Morse
has intra-letter gaps of 1 unit between symbols but inter-letter gaps of 3
units. Since I render one letter per cycle, there's no "next letter" — but
without a trailing dark, the last symbol of the letter would run straight into
the first symbol of the next cycle (`Mo(A)` = `·−` would become `·−·−·−`,
reading as `KW` to anyone actually decoding). I add a 3-unit trailing dark as
part of the period so each cycle is cleanly delimited. Small thing, but
without it the quiz's Morse answers would be ambiguous.

**The notation formatter has to suppress the group count for rhythms where it's
meaningless.** `Fl(1) W 4s` is wrong — a single flash is just `Fl W 4s`. Same
for `Oc(1)`, and `F` never takes a count at all. But `Iso` and `Q` *can* take
a count (`Iso(2)` = two on/off pairs per cycle). The formatter special-cases
this: count > 1 is shown for Fl/Oc/Iso/Q, suppressed for F, and Mo always
shows the letter instead of a count. Got this wrong on the first pass and
Tybee Island rendered as `F(1) W 8s`, which is nonsense.

**The period formatting has a half-second edge.** Cape Hatteras is `Fl W 7.5s`
— the only preset with a non-integer period. My first formatter did
`${period}s` and printed `7.5s` fine, but also printed `4.0s` for integer
periods instead of `4s`. Added a `formatPeriod` that strips trailing `.0`, so
integers render clean and halves render as `7.5s`. The Node test caught this
directly — there's an assertion `formatPeriod(4) === "Fl W 4s"` and
`formatPeriod(7.5) === "Fl W 7.5s"`.

**I verified every preset's characteristic against primary sources before
writing them down.** The USCG Light List (via the U.S. Lighthouse Society's
historical database) and Wikipedia were cross-checked: Portland Head `Fl W 4s`,
Heceta Head `Fl W 10s`, Bodie Island `Fl(2) W 30s`, Cape Hatteras `Fl W 7.5s`,
Point Reyes `Fl W 5s`, Tybee Island `F W` (fixed — genuinely steady, no flash),
Pigeon Point `Fl W 10s`, Minot's Ledge `Fl(1+4+3) W 45s`. A common failure
mode for "real data" projects is trusting the first search result; several
Facebook posts and AI summaries listed wrong characteristics (e.g. Heceta Head
as `Fl(2) 20s`, which contradicts the actual USCG entry). Went to the light
list itself.

**Left out of scope:** sector lights (where a single lamp shows different
colors across different arcs — red for danger, white for the safe channel,
green for the other side), the `Al` alternating-color rhythm, fixed-and-flashing
(`FFl`) which St. Augustine actually uses, range lights (front/rear pairs you
align to stay in the channel), and a "decode by ear" mode that would play the
flashes as audio beeps. Sector lights are the most interesting follow-up —
they're how a single lighthouse encodes *spatial* information (where you are
relative to the channel) into a *temporal* signal, which is a lovely trick.

**Verification.** 101 engine assertions pass in `test_engine.js`: every
preset's timeline sums to exactly its period, every notation string matches a
hand-written expectation, and `isLitAt(t)` is checked at multiple phases for
all six rhythm types (including a worked Morse-A timing case broken down by
unit). The browser itself rendered correctly on load — confirmed via DOM
snapshot (notation, plainwords, all 8 presets, status bar updating live) and a
vision-checked screenshot of the lit stage canvas (lamp halo, sweeping beams,
star field, lighthouse with red bands). The IAB screenshot channel was flaky
this session — recurring "previous screenshot still completing after timeout"
wedges after the first capture, the same class of intermittent instability
ballot-lab and loom-drafter recorded — so quiz-mode verification was done via
DOM (choices render, answering increments score, feedback text correct:
`Mo(S) W 6s` → "Morse code for S (···)") rather than additional screenshots.
