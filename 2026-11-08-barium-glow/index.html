Every shadow here is Lambert–Beer: each centimetre of matter multiplies the beam by e−μt, and μ falls as the rays harden — slowly for flesh, dramatically for bone and brass, because the photoelectric effect loves heavy atoms. Voltage buys penetration, current buys photons, and the plate only counts what arrives: too few and the grain never resolves, far too many and the film saturates black.

Attenuation coefficients are set by hand at 60 kVp and stretched with a photoelectric (≈E−3) term over a Compton floor; tube output grows with kVp². Grain is Poisson: the plate samples real photon counts per pixel, so noise fades as 1/√count. The hand, the paperclip and the nail are procedural thickness maps. Röntgen's first medical radiograph — his wife's hand, 22 December 1895 — needed fifteen minutes of this same arithmetic. Wilhelm Röntgen ↗
