On October 24, 1946, a camera on a V-2 launched from White Sands took the first photograph of Earth from space, at about 105 km altitude. This is a procedural camera experiment inspired by that image, not a reconstruction of its terrain or lens.

Each pixel casts a ray toward a sphere of radius 6,371 km. A ray that hits becomes Earth; a ray that misses becomes sky. Horizon dip is acos(R / (R + h)); distance to the horizon is √(h(2R + h)). No refraction, terrain or lens distortion. The camera points seven degrees below the geometric horizon, so it follows the limb as you climb. GEO HEIGHT is an altitude comparison, not a view from a geostationary orbit above White Sands.
