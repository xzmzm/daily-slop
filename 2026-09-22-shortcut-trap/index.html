How the numbers work ↓
The model, not a forecast +

Five directed links. S → A and B → T each take f / 100 minutes, where f is the number of drivers assigned to that road. S → B and A → T each take 45 minutes. The shortcut is one-way, A → B. Its selected travel time is constant.

Two different questions. “Every driver” solves a continuous-flow Wardrop equilibrium. “Coordinator” minimizes total travel time; that assignment need not be stable when people choose for themselves. Fractions are allowed in the model. Displayed driver counts are rounded.

A deliberately tiny world. Demand is fixed, not induced by road building. No signals, spillback, queues, route-learning dynamics, or real city data. Cars are sample markers, not individual simulated agents. The shortcut can have zero mathematical travel time even though its markers still move visibly.

Background: Easley & Kleinberg, Networks, Crowds, and Markets, chapter 8. Built for the closing day of European Mobility Week 2026.
