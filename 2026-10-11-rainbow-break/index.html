Rainbow Break

CBS colour TV sent pictures in black and white, one colour at a time; a disk spinning at 1,440 rpm in front of the tube painted each field red, green or blue. Your eye did the mixing. Here is where the eye fails at it.

144 fields, 24 pictures405 lines, 144 fields a second. Three colour fields and two interlaced halves make 24 complete colour pictures every second.
Four months on airThe Supreme Court upheld the FCC in May 1951; CBS broadcast colour from June 25 until the Korean War halted colour set production that October.
Still spinningRCA's compatible system became the 1953 standard. Colour wheels came back in single-chip DLP projectors, rainbows included.

The eye is modelled as four cascaded 12 ms low-pass stages; each field's light is weighed by how much of that response it falls inside, then placed where it landed on a retina that may be moving. Slow motion slows the eye too, so you see the bare fields. Field rates stop at 48/s to avoid fast full-screen colour flashing.
