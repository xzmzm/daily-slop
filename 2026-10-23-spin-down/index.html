What this little machine assumes +

On October 23, 2001, Apple introduced the iPod with a 5 GB hard drive and advertised up to 20 minutes of shock protection. Read-ahead memory can keep feeding a decoder while the disk rests.

This experiment stores playable seconds, with a 2-second continuous-read control and a 2- or 20-minute read-ahead player. Both start preloaded. A sleeping disk wakes with 30 seconds left (12 seconds for the small buffer), takes 2 seconds to spin up, then reads 40 seconds of music per second until full. These are chosen teaching parameters.

Blocking reads is a complete, temporary read outage. Skipping chooses a track outside both reserves, discarding their contents. Disk-awake time includes spinning up and waiting through an outage; it is not a battery-life estimate. Optional sound is an original synthesized loop from the read-ahead player.
