The Web, As a Markov Chain

A surfer lands on a page and keeps clicking links. The fraction of all eternity she spends on page i is that page's rank — no words read, only arrows followed. Click two nodes to add or remove a link, then re-run the iteration.

Rank Board

The surfer never reads a word: Yahoo wins because six pages point at it, and the pages pointing at it are themselves pointed at. A link is a vote; a vote from a ranked page weighs more.

The Damping Knob

Power iteration converges at the rate of the second eigenvalue: |λ₂(G)| = d·|μ₂(A)| ≤ d. Small d converges fast but flattens opinion; d → 1 preserves the pure link structure — and stops converging at all on cycles.

Bench graph

The paper's number. On 322 million links the rank vector converged in 52 iterations. The worst-case factor is d, and 0.85⁵² = 2.14×10⁻⁴ — exactly the tolerance you would expect.
