SUNDAY, NOVEMBER 18, 1883 · FIVE MERIDIANS · 4 MINUTES PER DEGREE

Two Noons.

Until this Sunday every town kept its own noon, the moment the sun crossed its own meridian. At standard noon the railroads of the United States and Canada reset their station clocks to one of five meridians.
Run the day. Towns east of their meridian were already past noon and set their clocks back, so noon struck twice. Towns west of it jumped forward and lost those minutes.

    The day: On Sunday, November 18, 1883, the railroads of the United States and Canada replaced dozens of railroad times with five standard times based on the meridians 60°, 75°, 90°, 105° and 120° west of Greenwich. Station clocks were set to standard noon as it occurred. Congress did not make standard time law until the Standard Time Act of 1918. This page treats each zone as ±7.5° around its meridian, starts every clock on its town's local mean time, and uses city-centre longitudes, not the observatories that actually sent the signals. Real 1883 zone edges followed railroad division points, and some towns (Detroit most famously) kept local time for years.
