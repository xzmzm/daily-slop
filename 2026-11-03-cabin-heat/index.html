HOW THE CABIN WORKS

Heat comes in, heat radiates out.
The cabin sits where the two meet.

In space there is no air outside to carry heat away, so the only exit is radiation, which grows with the fourth power of temperature. Heat comes from the dog and the instruments all the time, and from the Sun for the two-thirds of each 103.7-minute orbit spent in daylight. Torn insulation lets more sunlight in. The attached core stage soaks up sunlight beside the cabin and blocks part of its view of cold space. This bench is one lumped node with invented constants, tuned only so the flown case crosses 40 °C a few hours in. It is a lesson in balance, not a reconstruction of the real spacecraft.
