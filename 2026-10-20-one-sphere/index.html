One sphere.

Utzon's 1957 sketch had sails of no particular geometry, and for four years nobody could say how to build them. His answer: cut every shell from the same ball, 75 m in radius. Then every rib bends the same way. Drag to turn. Lift the shells off the sphere, then switch back to the sketch.

HOW THE SPHERE HELPS

Same ball, same bend,
same moulds.

Each shell here is a fan of ribs that leave a pedestal and curl upward. Cut from a sphere, every rib is a piece of a 75 m circle, so a 4.5 m rib segment has the same curve wherever it sits on the roof, and the segments can be cast in repeating moulds. Draw the sails free-form and each rib turns at its own rate along its length. The count above treats two segments as interchangeable only when their radius matches at both ends to within half a metre. Shell sizes and positions are stylised, not survey data.
