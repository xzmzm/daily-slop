    Why a freighter with a hump

    When the first 747 — City of Everett — rolled out of the new Everett plant on September 30, 1968, Boeing expected supersonic transports to take over passenger flying. The 747 was designed to lose its passengers gracefully: the flight deck went up onto the hump so the nose could swing open for straight-in cargo loading, and the type became the freighter that carried the air-cargo age. The plant those 50,000 “Incredibles” built is still the largest building in the world by volume; the last 747 left it in January 2023.
