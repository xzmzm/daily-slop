61 YEARS AGO TODAY · THE NORTHEAST BLACKOUT

Set point.

Five 230 kV lines carried Niagara power from Sir Adam Beck to Toronto. In 1963 a backup relay on one of them was set to trip at 375 MW, and nobody on shift knew the number. At 5:16:11 pm the line was carrying about 356 MW when a short swing pushed it over. Its load moved onto the other four, and they tripped one after another.

How the board works

Each line's flow comes from a DC power flow: power splits across parallel paths in inverse proportion to their reactance. When a line trips, its flow has nowhere to go but the remaining paths. An island that cannot make at least 92 % of its load loses frequency and goes dark.

What's real

The 375 MW setting, the five Beck–Toronto lines, the 356 MW reading, 5:16:11 pm, about 30 million people and up to 13 hours without power. Afterwards Ontario Hydro raised the settings on all five relays.

What's invented

The buses, reactances, other line limits, generation, the size of the swing and the event times after the first trip are round numbers chosen to echo the event. This is a teaching model, not the 1965 network.
