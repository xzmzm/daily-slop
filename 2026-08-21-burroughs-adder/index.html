How the 1888 Burroughs Mechanism Works

1. Differential Stop-Rack: Pressing a key pushes a steel pin down into the path of the sector rack. Pulling the crank lets the rack drop under spring tension until it hits that pin ($0 \le d \le 9$).

2. Print Hammer Strike: At full handle pull, spring-loaded type hammers strike the inked ribbon against paper tape, printing the entered digits.

3. Dashpot-Governed Return: An oil-filled dashpot regulates the return stroke speed. As the rack returns up to zero, it turns the accumulator pinion forward by $+d$ teeth. If it rolls past 9, it trips a carry latch.

4. Sequential Carry Ripple: A right-to-left sweep cam ripples across decades, nudging each primed column by $+1$.

The 1888 Burroughs Adding Machine

On August 21, 1888, American inventor William Seward Burroughs was granted U.S. Patent No. 388,116 for his Calculating Machine.

Before Burroughs, bank clerks worked grueling 12–14 hour days adding massive columns of numbers in heavy paper ledgers. A single error meant hours of re-checking. Burroughs, a former bank clerk whose own health broke down from bookkeeping fatigue, spent seven years developing the first reliable calculating and printing machine.

The Two Breakthroughs That Made It Practical

  • The Hydraulic Dashpot (The Oil Damper): Early machines broke down or miscalculated because human operators jerked the handle with erratic force, causing gear teeth to overshoot their stops by inertia ("momentum overthrow"). Burroughs placed a piston inside a cylinder of oil with a calibrated bleed port. No matter how violently the clerk pulled the crank, fluid viscosity limited gear acceleration to a safe, constant speed!
  • Total / Subtotal Register Reversal: To clear the accumulator and print the grand total (*), the machine reverses its gear mesh timing: it engages the accumulator pinions during the downstroke, winding the dials backward until they hit zero stops (which positions the racks and type hammers to the stored sum), then disengages before the return stroke—leaving the register cleanly zeroed!
