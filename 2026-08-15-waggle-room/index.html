how to read a waggle dance

A forager back from good flowers dances a figure-eight on the vertical comb, in the dark. Everything you need is in the middle run:

  • the angle of the waggle run from straight up is the angle of the flowers from the sun. Up the comb is toward the sun — gravity substitutes for the missing sky.
  • the duration of the waggling is the distance: t ≈ a + b·d in the hive's own dialect. For von Frisch's Carniolans, one second of waggling ≈ one kilometre. You can count waggles instead of timing: n ≈ 13·t.
  • too close (a few dozen metres) and the figure-eight collapses into a round dance: distance yes, direction no.

And the sun keeps moving — so for the same flowers, the dance slowly rotates through the day. The bee's clock subtracts it; von Frisch's student Lindauer proved she even compensates for the sun's overnight arc.

dialects

Distance-in-seconds is not universal: subspecies — even neighbouring hives — calibrate it differently, and the calibration is inherited, not learned.

the fine print, verified

  • 1973 — Karl von Frisch shared the Nobel Prize for decoding this, the only known symbolic language of an invertebrate.
  • the trick — inside a dark hive there is no sun to aim by, so evolution aimed by gravity: straight up the comb means toward the sun.
  • dialects persist — a Carniolan strain kept in Japan reportedly danced its ancestral Austrian calibration for generations after import.
  • reading in the dark — followers crowd behind the dancer with antennae on her abdomen; Michelsen's laser vibrometer later read the waggle right off the comb surface.
  • only winners dance — a poor nectar source is danced for by no one: the comb is a market and the dance is a buy signal.
