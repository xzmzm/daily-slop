221 YEARS AGO TODAY · WIND LIGHT FROM THE WEST

Break the line.

For a century, fleets fought in two parallel lines, and the losing side simply ran downwind. Nelson pointed his columns straight at the enemy line. His bows took raking fire while not one of his guns could bear, and he came out on the leeward side, where the enemy would have run. Pick an approach, press play, then try the other one.

HOW THE TABLE WORKS

The guns point sideways.
The wind decides who can leave.

A ship of the line carried its guns along its sides, so it could only fire at what lay roughly abeam, here within 25° of the beam. Sail straight at an enemy and none of your guns bear while all of theirs do, and their shot travels the length of your decks: a rake, scored here at three times the damage but harder to hit. Pass through their line and both of your broadsides rake the sterns and bows beside you. Fire is expected value, not dice, with hit fraction falling off over a few hundred metres. A Combined ship below half strength bears away downwind unless a British ship is sitting to leeward of it; at 15 points left a ship strikes. Fleets are scaled down (8 against 12, not 27 against 33) and the numbers are a toy, not a reconstruction.
