# Carryover build notes

## Why this project?

Today is 26 November 2026, the fourth Thursday of November, so it's Thanksgiving in the US. Most of the day's arguments happen at the oven door: when do you take the bird out? The usual rule is "165°F in the thickest part of the breast". Two things make it interesting to simulate. The bird keeps heating after it leaves the oven, and bacterial kill in lethality tables depends on time at temperature, not on one number. The page models both. It does **not** replace the USDA's advice for real turkeys: check with a food thermometer that the thickest part of the breast and the innermost parts of the thigh and wing reach at least 165°F ([FSIS](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/What-is-the-internal-temperature-and-cooking-time-for-turkey)).

Both facts are hard to see in a kitchen and easy to see in a heat simulation, so the page simulates them. Other candidates for the date were Carter opening Tutankhamun's tomb (1922) and InSight landing on Mars (2018). Nothing in the repo had touched cooking, and the earlier thermal project (Sputnik 2's cabin) was about getting rid of heat, not soaking it in.

## How it works

- **Geometry.** A 2D cross-section on a 5 mm grid. The body is an ellipse 25 × 18 cm for a 14 lb bird, scaled by the cube root of the weight. An empty cavity sits low inside it, which leaves about 6 cm of breast on top and a thin back below. The cavity air follows the outside air with a 50-minute lag.
- **Heat.** An explicit finite-difference conduction step every 10 s. Meat has k = 0.45 W/m·K and ρc = 1050 × 3500. Each face that touches air has a series conductance of `1 / (1/h + dx/2k)`. The oven uses h = 11 W/m²·K, which combines convection and radiation. Resting uses h = 6 for a loosely tented bird at 70°F. Skin cells are capped at 90°C while roasting, as a stand-in for evaporation, which holds a wet surface well below the oven temperature.
- **Kill.** Each cell sums `dt / D(T)` in log reductions, where `D(T) = D150 · 10^((150 − T)/10)` with T in °F, and D150 = 2.7 min / 7. That reproduces the familiar poultry table to within ~15%: 7 logs takes ~27 min at 140°F, 2.7 min at 150°F, ~50 s at 155°F and ~16 s at 160°F. Below 130°F the page counts no kill. "Model target met" means the least-killed cell reaches 7 logs by carving time. It is a statement about the simulation only.
- **Juice.** Each cell loses `0.4% × (peak °F − 120)` of its water, capped at 30%. The page averages that over all the meat. It is a rough linear fit, not a measured curve. The verdict calls ≤ 24% juicy and > 25.5% dry.
- **The plan.** Roast until the thermometer cell reads the pull temperature, rest for the chosen minutes, then carve. The carved view colours each cell by its peak temperature.

## Interesting notes

- The first version had no skin cap. The surface ran to nearly oven temperature, carryover came out at 16°F, and nearly every pull made the bird "dry". With the 90°C cap, carryover at the default thermometer spot is 9°F (165 → 174°F), which is within the 5–10°F cooks usually quote.
- The "coldest meat" readout went wrong as soon as the bird came out. On the counter the skin cools first and becomes the coldest meat, although it was cooked long ago. The readout now tracks the **slowest spot**, the cell with the least kill, with ties going to the colder cell. That's the cell the model's kill target depends on.
- The default thermometer spot is halfway between the skin and the cavity roof in the breast. It turned out to be the slowest spot, too, so for the default plan the thermometer and the purple ring overlap.
- Resting turned out to matter for the model's kill as well as juiciness. Pull at 145°F and carve at once, and the slowest spot gets only ~2.4–2.5 logs (2.4 in `sim.test.js`, 2.5 in the page, which checks the pull a little later because it steps in frame-sized batches). Pull at 145°F and rest 30 minutes, and it meets the model target easily, because it spends a long stretch in the 150s.
- The kill numbers grow enormous (thousands of logs) once meat sits above 160°F. The page shows anything past 100 as "100+ log" so the number doesn't look like a scoreboard.
- Out of scope: the thighs and wings (which the USDA's 165°F check also covers), stuffing, brining, and the real 3D shape. The 2D slice has no end faces, so it heats more slowly than a real bird. The oven h is tuned to make up for that, and the slice is meant for comparing plans, not for timing dinner.
- **Correction, same day.** The first version labelled a 150°F pull "Safe", and the first video said so too. A 2D slice with a fitted kill curve can't establish that a real turkey is safe. It ignores the thighs and wings, real shape and probe placement, and every number in it is a rough fit. The verdict now reads "model target met / missed" in neutral ink, never green, so it can't pass for an all-clear. A notice links the USDA's 165°F guidance, and the page flags any pull reading below 165°F. The video was re-recorded to match.
