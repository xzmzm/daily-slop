Nov 17, 1970: Luna 17 set the first rover on another world, and a five-man crew in Crimea drove it by television. The television was slow-scan: one still picture at a time, by the usual account about every 20 seconds.

04 / THE LOG

    FINISHED DRIVES

      The mission: Luna 17 landed in Mare Imbrium on November 17, 1970, and Lunokhod 1 rolled down its ramps a few hours later — 756 kg, eight wire-mesh wheels, two forward speeds. Five-man crews drove it in shifts from the deep-space station near Simferopol, off slow-scan television. It worked for 322 days and covered about 10.5 km. The terrain, turn rate, camera height and hazard distances here are this page's model, not mission data; the two speeds, the light time and the four frame periods are real, the periods being Lunokhod 2's documented settings.
